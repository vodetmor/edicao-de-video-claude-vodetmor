## Motion instructions to carry into the AE build

Use fast departures and smooth deceleration into clear destinations. Start by overlapping outgoing and incoming actions by 6–10 frames at 30 fps, then tune against the actual preview. These are proposed construction values, not measured reference keys. Keep one dominant action at a time. Let text settle briefly while a related reveal, cursor action, annotation or next transition carries motion forward. Avoid arbitrary wobble, drifting unreadable text, or moving every layer simultaneously.

Build camera and object motion separately: the camera connects the same spatial layout; each component performs its action within it. Preserve the lime cursor's identity as caret → border → handles → playhead → underline. Use motion blur during fast travel and sharp type at the reading destinations. No long dead pauses between sections. No six independent full-screen slide entrances.

Keep music clearly audible, with a brisk instrumental groove and restrained impacts on major direction changes. Place sounds against the visual timing rather than extending shots to fit a music phrase. Use separate music/effects stems and a clean 10-second ending. The earlier 18-second/nine-bar music structure is superseded.

## First motion proof

Before spending time on surface polish, render a full 10-second native AE motion pass with live type and simple shapes. The handoff from storyboard to parts to timeline is the key test. Compare the complete preview with the source at normal speed: focal changes, travel duration, overlapping secondary actions, readability and the pullback/exit should carry similar energy. A still storyboard cannot verify cadence. Tune any pause or crowded beat before final shading and sound polish.
