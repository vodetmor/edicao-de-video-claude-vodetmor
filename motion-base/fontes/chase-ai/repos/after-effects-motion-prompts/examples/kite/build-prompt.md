# KITE — final direction build prompt

New reusable consolidation of the initial build, later visual revision and final music direction. This was not the single prompt used to produce the final film. The saved earlier prompts and revision notes are in [history/](history/). Repository-relative reference and asset paths have been added.

```text
Create a 15-second launch promo for KITE, a fictional AI Kanban product, as an editable Adobe After Effects project using the connected local Higgsfield bridge. Deliver 1920×1080 at 30 fps.

The viewer should understand that KITE turns a brief into a plan, exposes dependencies and shows what can move next after a human approves the work.

Use references/pocket-world-styleboard.png as the Pocket World reference: a warm miniature desktop, ivory cardboard board, shallow compartments, blue/coral/yellow clay cards, inset paper labels and soft contact shadows. The chartreuse guide is a small clay kite with two eyes and a curling cord tail. Maya is a purple human cursor with a readable name tag. Keep text and product actions visually dominant.

Stage one connected board scene, using close-ups and pullbacks to direct attention. Use the prepared assets/desk-background.png, assets/board.png, assets/clay-card.png and assets/kite.png as separate background, board, card-surface and kite-body artwork. The delivered PNGs are RGB; isolate the board, card and kite using native AE masks rather than assuming an alpha channel; use native editable AE text, shapes, controls, masks, annotations, eyes, tail and animation. Keep the fixed-view material art convincing through modest camera pans and scale changes.

Most physical moves use one pose per two output frames, with brief anticipation, compression on contact and one controlled settle. Camera motion stays smooth. Stagger material arrival, text reveal, drawn accent and character reaction. Use quick camera handoffs and still reading holds.

0–1.5 s: Reveal “Big ideas.” and “Clear plan.” above a paper brief: “Launch our website Friday.” Maya presses “Make a plan” at 1.10 s.

1.5–3.6 s: Reveal the “Website launch” board with fixed columns “To do”, “Doing”, “Review”, “Done”. Blue “Design landing page”, coral “Draft launch copy” and yellow “QA checklist” arrive in To do. Ivory “Wireframes” is already in Done. Card bodies arrive first; titles follow.

3.6–4.6 s: Flip the “Later · Draft ready” tab and move coral into Review.

4.6–6.8 s: Move closer to the dependency. Draw a cord between blue and coral, close a small latch, reveal blue's “Blocked” label and “Waiting for approved copy”. Draw a small yellow circle around the blocked status. Let the guide inspect it, then look toward Maya. Hold the relationship long enough to understand.

6.8–8.8 s: Frame coral and Maya. Maya presses “Approve” at 7.55 s. Show an “Approved” stamp, reveal handwritten “Looks good.” and give the guide a restrained pleased reaction.

8.8–11.5 s: Coral reaches Done around 9.10 s and receives its check. Release the latch around 9.35 s; blue changes from Blocked to Ready while remaining unfinished in To do. Yellow stays in To do; Wireframes stays in Done. Reveal “Keep work moving.” and “Your move.” Keep Maya's label clear of all copy.

11.5–15 s: Pull back and reveal “Meet KITE.”, “Less busywork.”, “More momentum.” and “Join the waitlist” as separate beats. Finish visual motion by 12.6 s and hold to 15 s. Done contains coral and Wireframes; blue remains Ready in To do.

Sound: clearly audible instrumental background with warm bass, dry drums, sustained synth harmony and a restrained melodic phrase. Use tactile paper, soft impacts and mechanical latch effects. Let key actions lead with brief local music dips; resolve the ending with the KITE identity and a short musical tail. Keep music/effects separate.

Preserve existing projects. Check the actual render for readable copy, clean masks, correct state changes and character/cursor overlaps. Deliver a new AEP, collected media, separate audio stems and H.264 preview. Use local AE rendering, with no cloud video-generation job. Identify any remaining raster-art or verification limitations accurately.
```
