## Copy-ready prompt

Create a polished 15-second announcement for KITE, a fictional AI Kanban product, in Adobe After Effects using the Higgsfield After Effects bridge.

Use the supplied Pocket World styleboard as the visual reference. The viewer should understand: KITE turns a brief into a plan, reveals dependencies, and shows what can move next after a person approves the work.

ART DIRECTION
Build a charming miniature desktop world: a thick ivory cardboard board, shallow compartments, colorful clay task cards, inset paper labels, subtle fingerprints, soft contact shadows, and warm studio lighting. Use cornflower blue, tomato coral, butter yellow, chartreuse and cream. Keep the same slightly elevated three-quarter view and believable physical scale throughout.

The guide is a small chartreuse clay kite with two simple eyes and a flexible cord tail. Give it personality through curious tilts, attentive pauses and one small delighted reaction. Keep the board and important copy visually dominant. Use restrained desk props and generous empty space.

ASSETS AND AFTER EFFECTS BUILD
Prepare separate artwork for the clean background, board, clay cards, status tiles, button, latch and kite guide. Reuse the supplied reference to keep materials consistent. Use these assets in an editable layered After Effects composition, with live text, independent controls, masks and animated shadows. Keep camera travel modest so the prepared artwork retains convincing perspective. Match the reference's dimensional appearance closely; a textured asset's underlying geometry need not be editable.

Create a new project and a 1920 × 1080 master composition, exactly 15 seconds at 30 fps. Preserve the existing projects. Keep names, timing and major colors easy to change.

MOTION LANGUAGE
Make the objects behave like handmade stop-motion miniatures. Animate most clay and cardboard movement on twos: one pose per two output frames. Use short, purposeful moves, slight rotation, a small compression on contact, and a single settling wobble. Keep the camera smooth and the reading holds completely still.

Buttons visibly dent. Cards have weight. A thin cord and tiny latch make a dependency physical. The kite looks toward the next action before moving. Avoid continuous jitter or idle bouncing.

SEQUENCE
0–2 seconds — A small paper brief rests on the miniature desk: “Launch our website Friday.” A purple cursor labeled “Maya” presses the soft chartreuse “Make a plan” button. It compresses and releases.

2–5 seconds — Pull back as the task cards settle into a board titled “Website launch”, with four fixed columns: “To do”, “Doing”, “Review”, “Done”. Blue “Design landing page”, coral “Draft launch copy” and yellow “QA checklist” start in To do. Ivory “Wireframes” is already in Done. Near 4.4 seconds, a small cardboard tab flips to “Later · Draft ready” as coral moves into Review, clearly marking elapsed work time.

5–8 seconds — Move closer to the blue and coral cards. A cord links them, with a small latch holding the dependency closed. Blue displays “Blocked”. A paper label reads “Waiting for approved copy”. The kite leans in to inspect the latch, then looks toward Maya. Hold the relationship clearly.

8–9.5 seconds — Show coral “Draft launch copy” with an “Approve” button. Maya visibly presses it; the clay button dents. The kite watches. All resulting changes happen after this human approval.

9.5–11.5 seconds — Coral moves into Done and receives its checkmark. Then the latch opens and the blue card's status tile flips from “Blocked” to “Ready”. Blue stays unfinished in To do. Yellow remains in To do. Wireframes stays in Done, so Done now contains two cards. The kite gives one small pleased tilt.

11.5–15 seconds — Pull back to the final miniature board beside large “Meet KITE.” typography. Reveal “From brief to board.”, “Know what's next.” and a “Join the waitlist” button. Finish all movement by 12.5 seconds and hold the complete end frame for 2.5 seconds.

FINISH AND DELIVERY
Keep all listed copy exact and legible. Add gentle instrumental music with small cardboard scrapes, a soft button press and a latch click. No narration. Add no extra slogans or product claims.

Save the After Effects project, collected source assets, and an H.264 MP4 preview. Review the actual render for readability, material depth, clean compositing and correct approval-to-unblocking order. Fix visible issues before presenting the result.
