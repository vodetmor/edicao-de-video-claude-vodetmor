# After Effects Motion Prompts

Three finished motion demos from **Chase AI**, paired with the prompts, storyboards and revisions behind them. Made with Codex, the Higgsfield local bridge and Adobe After Effects.

**[Start with the reusable prompt template](PROMPT-TEMPLATE.md)** · [Chase AI Workflow](#1-chase-ai-workflow) · [KITE Pocket World](#2-kite-pocket-world) · [Whiteboard Loop](#3-whiteboard-loop)

## How to use this

1. Install [Adobe After Effects](https://www.adobe.com/products/aftereffects.html) and set up the [Higgsfield local bridge](https://github.com/higgsfield-ai/fnf-local-pluging-bridge-mcp#after-effects) for your assistant. Follow the bridge's current setup instructions and verify its live AE connection.
2. Pick an example below, or fill in the [storyboard template](templates/storyboard.md). Supply the listed reference images and approve the design, copy and timed beats.
3. Use the example's build prompt or the [general build template](templates/build.md) to construct and animate the editable pieces in After Effects.
4. Watch the render, then give [specific revisions](templates/revision.md) to timing, framing, copy or sound. For recreating a reference, use the [reference-video template](templates/reference-video.md).

A storyboard sets the direction; the build creates the layers, animation and sound. These examples include iteration, and a prompt does not guarantee an identical render.

Local AE editing and rendering through this bridge do not spend Higgsfield cloud-generation credits. Codex/model usage, Adobe access and any separately requested cloud image or video generation have their own usage or billing. See [sources and scope](SOURCES.md).

## The videos and their prompts

Each player below shows the film that its accompanying prompt describes. The repository videos are optimized 1080p previews; the Whiteboard master was rendered at 4K. Expand a prompt to copy it directly, or open its file for the complete context and attachments.

Attachment paths in each prompt are relative to that example's folder (`examples/chase-ai/`, `examples/kite/` or `examples/whiteboard/`). Open that folder as your assistant's workspace, or attach the files from it and tell the assistant where they are.

```text
Create a polished, editable Chase AI workflow animation in Adobe After Effects using the installed Higgsfield After Effects integration.

The film should demonstrate its own creation process:
idea → storyboard → prepared pieces → animation → revision → finished result.

FORMAT
10 seconds exactly, 1920×1080, 30 fps.
Build and render the animation locally in After Effects.

REFERENCES
Use:
• https://x.com/madmadhere/status/2098498233035305427 — motion and cadence reference.
• references/storyboard.png — visual direction.

The reference video contains a whiteboard animation above software and chat panels. Study the animation itself. Do not reproduce the surrounding demonstration interface, Higgsfield branding, or original whiteboard content.

Match its approximate rhythm: immediate activity, connected close-ups, brisk travel with smooth arrivals, overlapping reveals, a wider payoff, and a quick exit.

The Chase AI storyboard shows an earlier 18-second plan. Ignore its timestamps. Follow the 10-second sequence below.

CREATIVE DIRECTION
Make this feel like one continuous graphic world being constructed and directed in front of the viewer.

Use oversized condensed typography, confident compositions, shallow dimensional depth, crisp edges, and controlled shadows.

Proposed palette:
• Charcoal: #101116
• Warm white: #F6F4EB
• Electric lime: #CEFF42
• Cobalt: #3257F4
• Coral accent: #FF745E

Use CHASE AI as a plain typographic wordmark. Do not invent a logo symbol.

The recurring composition contains:
• White “MAKE IT MOVE” typography.
• A cobalt rectangle.
• A lime arc.
• A coral dot.

Reuse these same elements throughout. Their identities should remain recognizable as they appear in storyboard poses, separate into editable parts, animate in a preview, and receive an edit.

CONTINUITY DEVICE
A lime cursor carries the sequence:
typing caret → storyboard border → selection handles → timeline playhead → brand underline.

Make each transformation visually understandable. The viewer should be able to follow how one action causes the next.

MOTION LANGUAGE
Prioritize the continuous energy of the whiteboard reference.

Begin with motion already happening. Start the next action before the previous action has completely settled. Use overlapping text reveals, purposeful cursor movement, staggered object arrivals, and camera travel through the same workspace.

Use fast departures and smooth deceleration into readable destinations. As a starting point, overlap major handoffs by approximately 6–10 frames, then tune through playback.

Maintain one dominant action at a time. Let important words briefly settle while a related secondary action keeps the scene alive.

Avoid long static pauses, six independent slide entrances, arbitrary wobble, constant camera drift, or every element moving simultaneously. Preserve sharp text at reading moments and use motion blur during fast travel.

TIMED SEQUENCE

0.0–1.0 SECONDS — THE IDEA
Begin close to a prompt strip as “Make it move.” finishes typing.

Large copy:
“Think it.”

The lime caret immediately stretches into the leading edge of a storyboard frame. Follow it with the camera. Do not finish the opening and then pause before moving on.

1.0–2.5 SECONDS — SEE THE SEQUENCE
The frame opens into a connected strip of three storyboard poses showing the same MAKE IT MOVE design.

Large copy:
“See it.”

Reveal the tiles with a short stagger. Emphasize the center tile with the lime border while the neighboring tiles remain secondary.

As the center tile becomes readable, its border begins changing into selection handles. The next transformation should already be starting before the surrounding tiles finish settling.

2.5–4.5 SECONDS — PREPARE AND ASSEMBLE
Follow the selected tile laterally as the typography, cobalt rectangle, lime arc, and coral dot separate into shallow layers.

Show these as prepared, independently editable pieces. Use a brief assembly gesture to communicate reconstruction; do not imply that a flattened storyboard image automatically contains recoverable layers.

The pieces retain their relationship and assemble into a preview composition. Selection handles descend into timeline tracks below it.

Bend the camera movement downward to follow this transformation. The timeline should emerge from the pieces rather than arrive as an unrelated screen.

Keep this beat primarily visual. Do not add explanatory paragraphs or another large headline.

4.5–6.8 SECONDS — ANIMATE AND DIRECT
The lime edge becomes a playhead and moves across a simplified timeline. Its movement drives the MAKE IT MOVE preview above it.

Large copy:
“Make it move.”

Small contextual label:
“AFTER EFFECTS”

Show one clear animated action: the arc turns, the dot lands, and the typography arrives with a controlled settle.

A coral direction tag appears:
“Punchier.”

The cursor selects a small group of keyframes and moves them closer together. MOVE increases in scale. Briefly reset the playhead and replay the same action with visibly tighter timing and a stronger, controlled overshoot.

As the revision occurs, replace the large headline with:
“Dial it in.”

Keep the preview in the same focal area so the viewer understands that the existing animation has been edited.

6.8–9.0 SECONDS — REVEAL THE WHOLE PROCESS
Pull back to reveal the storyboard, prepared pieces, and timeline as connected parts of one workspace.

The components guide attention toward the brand composition as they finish their actions and begin clearing.

Resolve the wordmark by approximately 7.8 seconds:
“CHASE AI”

Reveal the supporting line by approximately 8.0 seconds:
“Your idea. In motion.”

The lime playhead becomes the underline. Its drawing motion and the remaining purposeful object exits keep the frame active while the brand copy is readable.

Omit file-format tabs and additional instructional labels.

9.0–10.0 SECONDS — FINISH WITH MOMENTUM
The remaining shapes sweep away along the lime line.

Keep the brand readable until approximately 9.5 seconds, then complete a short cursor-led wipe suitable for handing back to tutorial footage.

Finish cleanly at 10 seconds. Do not append a frozen end card or extra black frames.

SOUND
Include clearly audible instrumental background music from the start: a brisk groove with warm bass, dry percussion, and restrained synth accents.

Add selective synchronized effects for:
• The typing/caret transformation.
• Storyboard settling.
• Pieces separating and tracks forming.
• The “Punchier.” direction.
• The revised movement.
• The final underline and wipe.

Use sound to emphasize the main actions. Avoid a whoosh on every movement. Let the music remain present, with brief dips for important foreground cues.

No narration. Deliver separate music and effects stems. Use original synthesis or appropriately licensed available audio, and record its source.

AFTER EFFECTS CONSTRUCTION
Inspect the current project and preserve existing work. Save this as a separate Chase AI project.

Build typography, graphic shapes, borders, selection handles, timeline tracks, keyframes, and cursor elements with native editable AE text and shapes.

Reuse a shared MAKE IT MOVE composition wherever possible so the storyboard, preview, and revision remain consistent.

Keep camera movement separate from local object animation. Use parented layers and controlled shallow perspective to maintain spatial continuity.

Organize the project with clear composition and layer names. Keep copy and colors straightforward to change. Do not flatten the storyboard image into the finished video.

VERIFICATION AND DELIVERY
First render a complete 10-second motion pass with simple native shapes and live typography.

Compare its pacing with the whiteboard reference at normal playback speed. Check the speed of travel, short reading settles, overlapping actions, connected framing, and final pullback.

Fix dead pauses, crowded beats, unclear transformations, or unreadable copy before adding final surface and sound polish. Continue through the finished render.

Inspect the complete final video, including transitions and the ending. Check for clipping, missing assets, expression errors, blank frames, and audio truncation.

Deliver:
• The editable After Effects project.
• A 10-second MP4.
• Any required assets.
• Separate music and effects stems.
• Brief editing notes.

The success criterion is a clear workflow with the reference’s sense of momentum—not merely attractive individual frames.
```

```text
Create a 15-second launch promo for KITE, a fictional AI Kanban product, as an editable Adobe After Effects project using the connected local Higgsfield bridge. Deliver 1920×1080 at 30 fps.

The viewer should understand that KITE turns a brief into a plan, exposes dependencies and shows what can move next after a human approves the work.

Use references/pocket-world-styleboard.png as the Pocket World reference: a warm miniature desktop, ivory cardboard board, shallow compartments, blue/coral/yellow clay cards, inset paper labels and soft contact shadows. The chartreuse guide is a small clay kite with two eyes and a curling cord tail. Maya is a purple human cursor with a readable name tag. Keep text and product actions visually dominant.

Stage one connected board scene, using close-ups and pullbacks to direct attention. Use the prepared assets/desk-background.png, assets/board.png, assets/clay-card.png and assets/kite.png as separate background, board, card-surface and kite-body artwork. The delivered PNGs are RGB; isolate the board, card and kite using native AE masks rather than assuming an alpha channel; use native editable AE text, shapes, controls, masks, annotations, eyes, tail and animation. Keep the fixed-view material art convincing through modest camera pans and scale changes.

Most physical moves use one pose per two output frames, with brief anticipation, compression on contact and one controlled settle. Camera motion stays smooth. Stagger material arrival, text reveal, drawn accent and character reaction. Use quick camera handoffs and still reading holds.

0–1.5 s: Reveal “Big ideas.” and “Clear plan.” above a paper brief: “Launch our website Friday.” Maya presses “Make a plan” at 1.10 s.

1.5–3.6 s: Reveal the “Website launch” board with fixed columns “To do”, “Doing”, “Review”, “Done”. Blue “Design landing page”, coral “Draft launch copy” and yellow “QA checklist” arrive in To do. Ivory “Wireframes” is already in Done. Card bodies arrive first; titles follow.

3.6–4.6 s: Flip the “Later · Draft ready” tab and move coral into Review.

4.6–6.8 s: Move closer to the dependency. Draw a cord between blue and coral, close a small latch, reveal blue's “Blocked” label and “Waiting for approved copy”. Draw a small yellow circle around the blocked status. Let the guide inspect it, then look toward Maya. Hold the relationship long enough to understand.

6.8–8.8 s: Frame coral and Maya. Maya presses “Approve” at 7.55 s. Show an “Approved” stamp, reveal handwritten “Looks good.” and give the guide a restrained pleased reaction.

8.8–11.5 s: Coral reaches Done around 9.10 s and receives its check. Release the latch around 9.35 s; blue changes from Blocked to Ready while remaining unfinished in To do. Yellow stays in To do; Wireframes stays in Done. Reveal “Keep work moving.” and “Your move.” Keep Maya's label clear of all copy.

11.5–15 s: Pull back and reveal “Meet KITE.”, “Less busywork.”, “More momentum.” and “Join the waitlist” as separate beats. Finish visual motion by 12.6 s and hold to 15 s. Done contains coral and Wireframes; blue remains Ready in To do.

Sound: clearly audible instrumental background with warm bass, dry drums, sustained synth harmony and a restrained melodic phrase. Use tactile paper, soft impacts and mechanical latch effects. Let key actions lead with brief local music dips; resolve the ending with the KITE identity and a short musical tail. Keep music/effects separate.

Preserve existing projects. Check the actual render for readable copy, clean masks, correct state changes and character/cursor overlaps. Deliver a new AEP, collected media, separate audio stems and H.264 preview. Use local AE rendering, with no cloud video-generation job. Identify any remaining raster-art or verification limitations accurately.
```

### 3. Whiteboard Loop

**12 seconds · 30 fps · Silent**

One collaboration board with eight notes, editable lettering, animated cursors and connected camera moves that pull back into a loop.

[Watch / download MP4](https://github.com/cth9191/after-effects-motion-prompts/raw/refs/heads/main/media/videos/whiteboard.mp4) · **[Open the build prompt](examples/whiteboard/build-prompt.md)** · [Example guide](examples/whiteboard/README.md)

This is **our executed adaptation** of the visible whiteboard reference, not the original creator's recovered full prompt. Our approved version runs for 12 seconds.

[Saved production prompt](examples/whiteboard/original-production-prompt.md) · [Master board, storyboard and exact-copy plan](examples/whiteboard/README.md#attachment-authority)

<details>
<summary>Copy the build prompt</summary>

```text
Create an editable 12-second whiteboard animation in Adobe After Effects using the installed Higgsfield After Effects integration.

Use these supplied files:

- references/master-whiteboard.png: authority for the finished design, layout, colors, proportions, note tilts, and relative positions.
- references/six-beat-storyboard.png: authority for shot order, framing, and intended motion. Its captions and time labels are production notes, outside the animation.
- references/shot-plan.md: authority for exact note copy, handwriting, names, and interface labels. Typed copy takes priority over lettering in generated images.

Keep the master design consistent throughout the film. All close-ups must show the same continuous board. Recreate its eight notes and five collaborator cursors. Use our specified “Mobile offline mode” wording.

Create a composition named “Whiteboard Loop v1” at 3840 × 2160, 30 fps, exactly 12 seconds / 360 frames. Inspect the current AE project and preserve existing work in a separate working iteration.

Build the artwork first. Reconstruct note papers, folded corners, arrows, yellow highlight circle, dotted canvas, reaction badges, cursor pointers, and interface graphics with native editable shapes and paths. Use live text for wording. Small avatar photographs may remain image assets. Group each note’s paper, title, body, and reaction badge into one clearly named component, with independent editable content for each note. Keep text fully legible within its paper.

Render the complete static board and compare it with the master. Correct layout, copy, missing elements, colors, and proportions before adding motion. Continue into animation after these checks.

Use one 2D pan-and-zoom controller for the board, keeping component animation separate from the board movement. The toolbar belongs to the board and moves out of frame during close-ups, returning during the wide view. Preserve the flat appearance and the master’s note tilts.

Follow these six beats:

1. 0–1 s — Begin with the empty dotted canvas and board interface. Bring notes and collaborator cursors into place in a stagger.
2. 1–3 s — Push into the yellow AI-powered search note. Reveal its text progressively; bring Sofia into position. Finish the reveal early enough for a readable hold.
3. 3–5 s — Pan to the lavender Smart notifications note. Move Mina purposefully toward it, reveal its text, then hold.
4. 5–7.5 s — Move down to Unified workspace. Draw the yellow circle along its path and introduce the associated reaction and annotation.
5. 7.5–10.5 s — Pull back to the full eight-note board. All remaining copy and annotations are complete. Settle the view and hold it for at least the final second of this beat.
6. 10.5–12 s — Shrink the notes away with staggered exits, clear annotations and cursors, and restore the opening framing. Return to the empty-board state by the final frame for a clean loop.

Preserve these beat boundaries. Allow overlap inside them, while keeping arrivals, text reveals, and reading holds deliberate. Use brisk travel with an early speed peak and a smooth deceleration into stable holds. Start with 77.15% incoming influence on suitable arrival keys, and tune the outgoing influence, velocity, and timing to achieve that motion. Use a small number of purposeful keyframes and enable motion blur for fast movement. Keep settled type sharp. Secondary movement should follow the storyboard.

Keep the project easy to edit: independent note text, useful color controls, clear component names, and one board movement control. Retain the native paths for drawing and revising annotations. Use the installed integration and native AE features.

Inspect rendered frames at each hero close-up, during fast transitions, in the full-board view, and across the loop boundary. Review the complete animation at playback speed. Fix text clipping, soft close-ups, disconnected parts, expression errors, missing assets, blank frames, awkward holds, and the final-to-first transition. Test a longer note title in a temporary duplicate and check that its layout and animation remain usable.

Deliver the saved AEP, required assets, a complete 12-second MP4 preview, and brief editing notes. Verify the exported file’s dimensions, duration, frame rate, and playback. Describe any remaining differences from the storyboard honestly.
```
